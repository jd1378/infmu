      How it works

      There are no audio files here. Everything you hear is composed and synthesized in your browser, one sample at a time, from the seed.

      The seed plans an endless journey

      The seed picks a chain of movements (their length is yours to choose). Each has a style, a key, a tempo and a song form, and builds from quiet to a peak near its end. Keys move to close neighbours, styles to related ones, and a movement often rings out into a few seconds of rest before the next begins.

      Every movement has its motifs

      Each movement invents a hook in its style's idiom, shaped around one climax, and builds it into phrases the way songwriters do: question and answer, statement and development, a rising sequence. Every chorus restates it on the movement's own lead voice, and each return grows by one thing: a line answering it, a new layer, the hook doubled at the peak. Each style's grooves are written the way a producer programs them, and silence is written in too.

      Played by a band

      The parts a person would play are performed: melodies swell, breathe and are ornamented when they return, players drift a little around the beat and play softer under the melody, guitarists slide and hammer on, a pianist pedals, and a drummer plays with a feel of their own, every stroke and fill a little different. The tempo breathes too: a band pushes into its peak and slows into its last chord. Drum machines keep their exact grid.

      Sound from code

      Drums and hand percussion, pianos, guitars through an amp model, strings, an organ, mallets, bells, flute, saxophone, brass, a choir, a hummed voice and synthesizers are all computed in background workers, from physical and spectral models fitted to recordings of the real instruments. Each section is mixed in stems by measurement (ITU loudness) and leveled to its place in the movement's arc, in a room of its own with the faint noise floor of a recording, then played gaplessly with its reverb tail overlapping the next.

      The dial

      The ring is the circle of fifths, turned so the key's home note sits at the top. The shape inside is the chord playing now; sparks are notes, a line traces the melody. It reads the same score the synthesizer plays, so what you see is what you hear.

      Versions

      The composer improves in new versions, but old ones never change: a seed is tied to the version that played it, and links carry it. New seeds use the latest version; Choose seed lets you pick another.
